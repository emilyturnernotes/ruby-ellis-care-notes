Start by Gathering Your Paperwork

The first job is to find the documents that prove who owns the vehicle and what its history looks like. Having these ready makes the process much smoother.

Look for your V5C registration certificate, which is commonly called the logbook. If you cannot find it, check with the DVLA before going any further, as you will need it to transfer ownership. If your car has an MOT certificate, bring that too, along with any service history you have, such as receipts or a service book. A full set of records can help show the vehicle has been looked after.

Also collect your spare keys. A missing key is not always a dealbreaker, but it is worth knowing about in advance. If you still owe money on the car, find out the settlement figure from your finance provider so there are no surprises when the sale is agreed.

Be Honest About the Car's Condition

Before you visit, take a walk around the vehicle and make a short list of its condition. Note any dents, scratches, warning lights, or parts that do not work properly. You do not need to fix everything, but telling the buyer about known faults from the start builds trust and avoids awkward conversations later.

Give the inside a quick tidy as well. Remove your personal belongings, check the boot and glovebox, and vacuum the seats if you have time. A clean car is easier to inspect, and it gives a good first impression, even though the buyer will assess the vehicle on its mechanical and cosmetic merits rather than its shine.

Write Down the Basic Details

Keep a simple note of the make, model, year, engine size, colour and current mileage. Check the reading on the dashboard and compare it with the figure on your MOT certificate. If there is a difference, be ready to explain it. A quick note of any modifications, recent work such as a new battery or tyres, and the number of previous owners will also help.

These details matter because they give the buyer an accurate starting point. They also help you spot whether the information you have been given matches what you see on the day.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Several small errors can cause frustration when selling a car. The most common is forgetting to remove the number plates if you plan to keep them, so check this before you hand over the vehicle. Another is cancelling insurance or road tax too early, which can leave you exposed if the sale takes longer than expected. Keep your insurance active until the ownership transfer is complete.

People also sometimes accept an offer without reading the paperwork. Take a few minutes to look at what you are signing. If something is unclear, ask before you agree.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an MOT to sell my car?

Not always. A car does not need a valid MOT to be sold, but it can affect the price and how easily the buyer can take the vehicle on the road. Bringing your current MOT certificate, if you have one, helps the buyer understand the car's condition.

What if I cannot find my V5C logbook?

You can apply to the DVLA for a replacement. It is best to start this process early, as you will need the document to transfer ownership. Speak to the buyer before your appointment if you are unsure how to proceed.

Can I sell a car that is not running?

Many buyers will consider vehicles that are not in working order. Be honest about the problem when you call, and describe the symptoms as clearly as you can. This helps set realistic expectations for both sides.
